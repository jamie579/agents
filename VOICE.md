# Jamie B. Smith: Portable Voice Authority

This file is the public, self-contained summary of the private `academic-writing-jamie` and `decontamination` skills that the agents in this repository expect to load. Use it when an agent from this repository is asked to draft, edit, or judge prose in Jamie B. Smith's voice and the private skills are unavailable. When you use it, label the result `VOICE CALIBRATION: PORTABLE`. If you are writing for someone else, treat it as a worked example of what a voice authority contains and build the equivalent for your author.

It is generated from a private source and overwritten on every publish; open an issue rather than editing it here.

## The writer

Jamie B. Smith is a nurse academic with an intensive-care background at Charité – Universitätsmedizin Berlin, working across nursing philosophy, critical posthumanism and feminist new materialism, and health-workforce research. He writes in British English. Sole-authored work uses “I”; collaborative work uses “we”. The politics is specific: suspicious of explanations that individualise structural problems, of resilience discourse that relocates institutional failure into the worker, and of hero narratives that make ordinary collective labour disappear.

## Ten decisions the prose makes

1. **Start where the thinking starts.** The first sentence meets an object, scene, contradiction, or precise problem. It does not announce that a topic matters or rehearse a field history. Concrete detail is not orientation by itself: an opening scene must establish, or immediately disclose, the question that makes it consequential.
2. **Make the argument visible.** The paper takes a position. “I argue”, “I call this”, “we show” are ordinary. Claims stay proportionate to evidence, and caution never becomes evasiveness.
3. **Use theory as an instrument.** A concept enters where it changes what can be seen, distinguished, or done, with the exact term and, for close claims, the textual location. No roll-call of theorists as credentials.
4. **Define through discrimination.** A definition separates the concept from its nearest neighbours and shows what follows. A coined term arrives after the reader has felt the need for it.
5. **Ground abstraction in material practice.** A handover, a pain scale, a triage category, a prescribing system's default, a pair of socks: these are where the theory is made answerable, not decorations added afterwards.
6. **Name mechanisms and arrangements.** Critique names who or what acts, through which classification, resource decision, protocol, or history.
7. **Let wit perform analytic work.** Dry humour and plain speech puncture solemnity when the joke is also a discriminator. If a line could be removed without loss to the reasoning, it is decoration.
8. **Vary rhythm by argumentative job.** Long clause-rich sentences build; short declaratives land. Paragraphs are as long as the pressure they carry.
9. **Hedge with purpose.** One accurate hedge where the epistemic status requires it. No stacked qualifiers, no manufactured confidence.
10. **End by testing the contribution.** The close answers the question, states what changes, and may test whether the paper has performed what it describes. It never replays the abstract.

## How it sounds

Every quotation is verbatim from the 2026 anchors.

- **The first sentence risks something.** A verdict: “Consider that The Ethics is the most absurd, obtuse and severe text in Western philosophy.” A timed scene: “At 4:18 on a Tuesday in February, Mr Jones presents to A&E with an infected diabetic foot ulcer.” A reversal: “Theory arrives late to a scene that nurses have been improvising daily.”
- **Long build, short landing.** “The Ethics defeats this expectation.” “An event is forming.” “The vexation is the teaching.” “This becoming is not heroic. It is exhausting.” About a quarter of the sentences are ten words or fewer; the mean is around twenty.
- **Definition inside the sentence.** “camp (not subcultural taste but stylised excess performed with affectionate seriousness)”; “laetitia (joy as the passage to greater power to act)”.
- **Colons deliver.** “The triage system assigns him green: non-urgent.” “The sublime says: how vast. Camp says: look how much we can do with this.”
- **Semicolons pair a claim with its turn.** “The precision applied to jealousy is absurd; the absurdity is precise.” “The structure is identical; what it does to the practitioner is not.”
- **Em dashes are rare.** Fifteen in 8,000 words of one paper, two in 7,400 words of the other; each sets off a genuine aside, none glosses a term.
- **Contrast is native and rationed.** “not X but Y”, “rather than”, and chiasmus (“The rigour is the warmth; the deduction is the invitation.”) are his devices, at about one “rather than” per 500 words. Machine drafts reach one per 200. The device is the voice; the density is the tell.
- **The term arrives after the need.** “I call this device Euclidean drag.” comes after the device has been shown at work.
- **Plain speech inside density.** “as clear as mud”; “enjoy the ride”; “Spinoza was excommunicated for this.”; “a box to tick before going home”; “a spare pair of socks”.
- **Objects are exact.** “4 cm × 3 cm ulceration with purulent discharge, undermined edges, and cellulitis tracking proximally”; “white cell count: 14.2 × 10⁹/L”; “heat-stable, once-daily dosing”.
- **The reader is addressed and asked real questions.** “What happens when you read the Ethics from Part I through Part V in one sitting?” The next sentences answer.
- **Concessions are first person and specify what survives.** “I accept this.” “The gentrification risk is real, and I do not want to minimise it.”
- **Lists are scenes or discriminations, not tricolons.** “The bed manager checks capacity; the security guard continues their rounds; the junior doctor requests guidance.”
- **Endings turn.** “Rather than conclude, then, I invite.” “If the camp reading has worked, it has worked by doing what it describes; if it has not, no concluding summary will repair the deficit.”

## Two passages

Theory made answerable to a clinical object (*The Camp of Reason*):

> Consider the handover. A structured clinical handover (situation, background, assessment, recommendation) can be performed as a bureaucratic requirement, a box to tick before going home. The outgoing nurse recites the template; the incoming nurse transcribes it; the template is completed and the patient becomes a data set transferred between shifts. The same handover, enacted with enough care that the incoming nurse grasps the reasoning behind the selection — why these observations were weighted, what the assessment excluded and why, where the plan of care might need to bend — creates something different. The incoming nurse leaves not merely informed but equipped: able to extend the reasoning, adapt the plan, explain it to the patient, teach it to a student. The structure is identical; what it does to the practitioner is not. The first handover diminishes; it produces a nurse who knows what to do next. The second augments; it produces a nurse who understands why, and can therefore act when the protocol runs out. The difference is Spinozan: the passage from passive affect (receiving information whose cause remains external) to active affect (comprehending the reasoning and making it one's own).

A scene that produces its concept (*Staying With the Trouble*):

> Multiple temporalities converge in this moment. The 4-h emergency target exerts institutional pressure toward rapid discharge. Bacterial reproduction follows its own timeline, with infection advancing steadily through tissue. Mr Jones's anxiety about his abandoned belongings creates another urgency. The encounter composes with competing temporal demands while the wound continues to deteriorate. Standard intervention promises clinical improvement but almost guarantees self-discharge. No intervention risks progression to sepsis within days. The event of nursing happens precisely here: in the navigation of irreducible multiplicities that cannot be resolved through protocol alone. Such work is neither rare nor heroic; it is ordinary night-shift craft.

## Genre register

| Genre | Voice setting |
|---|---|
| Theoretical or philosophical paper | Maximum conceptual pressure; earned coinage, direct first person, close textual work, analytic wit. |
| Qualitative or posthuman empirical paper | Scenes and material details carry the analysis; theory must not erase participants or institutional conditions. |
| Quantitative paper or evidence synthesis | Precision is the voice. Conventional reporting where convention protects clarity; the signature appears in what is grouped and weighted, not in ornament. |
| Methods paper | Distinctions, decisions, consequences, limits. Procedural clarity outranks distinctiveness. Keep design-defined objects (estimand, allocation, prespecified contrast) distinct from practice-produced ones (delivered conditions, the realised comparison) and name the work connecting them. |
| Grant | Concrete problem, mechanism, proposed work, feasibility, deliverable. Theory earns space only by changing the design or question. |
| Reviewer response | Calm, direct, grateful where warranted, unservile. State the change, its location, and the reasoning. |

## Hard constraints when producing prose

These are rules for text you write, not review cues. When auditing the author's own human-written prose they become cues to classify in context.

1. No filler: “it is worth noting that”, “it is important to note that”, “this highlights the importance of”, “this underscores the need for”, “plays a crucial role”, “sheds light on”, “paves the way for”, “at the heart of”, “in recent years”, “a growing body of literature”, “has garnered significant attention”, “a rich tapestry of”, “in conclusion” as closure.
2. Inspect and usually replace: delve, multifaceted, pivotal, crucial, vital, adjectival nuanced, robust outside statistics, filler comprehensive, holistic, leverage, cornerstone, metaphorical tapestry or landscape, generic stakeholders, non-Kuhnian paradigm, trajectory, foster, bolster, underscore, unpack, metaphorical navigate, cutting-edge.
3. No paragraph opens with “Moreover”, “Furthermore”, “Additionally”, “Importantly”, “Notably”, “Significantly”, “Building on this”, or “It is also important to note”.
4. No sentence announces what a section will do instead of doing it. One compact architecture statement per paper (“In this paper, I make two claims.”) is allowed.
5. No paragraph ends by restating its point (“This highlights…”, “Taken together…”).
6. No conclusion replays the abstract or introduction.
7. Em dashes: none by default; at most one per 500 words, only for an aside commas would garble, never to gloss a term. Repair with a colon at clause end, parentheses, or a semicolon; do not let the aside migrate into a comma pile-up between subject and verb.
8. Contrast budget: “rather than”, “not X but Y”, trailing “, not Y” together at roughly one per 400 to 500 words; never two in a sentence or three in a paragraph. The repair is the positive claim.
9. Each epistemic disclaimer and each thesis formula appears once, in full, where it bites; no verbatim recurrence across section boundaries.
10. No numbered enumeration mirrored across consecutive sections; no content presented three times as prose, box, and table.
11. No § or ¶ glyphs in running prose; “and”, not “&”.
12. British English; German institutional terms retained, italicised, and glossed on first use.
13. No invented citation, quotation, page number, statistic, date, or clinical detail. Mark the gap (`[SOURCE NEEDED: …]` or `[AUTHOR INPUT NEEDED: …]`) instead of smoothing it into fluent generality.

## Argument discipline

Topic sentences make claims, and read in sequence they recover the argument. Evidence is read, not dropped: after a quotation, datum, or citation, say what it permits and what it does not. Synthesis groups sources by convergence, contradiction, or function. Reporting verbs mark stance (“shows”, “argues”, “suggests”, “concedes” are not interchangeable). The strongest objection is met where it bites, in the first person, and the paragraph ends by saying what survives. Scope (population, setting, condition, effect direction, uncertainty) travels with a claim into the discussion and abstract. Repetition is judged by function: a returning word or image is protected when each return introduces, complicates, tests, or closes an idea, and cut when it restates the same claim at the same level.

## The final test

For argumentative prose (introduction, theoretical or conceptual section, discussion, abstract, conclusion): if a careful academic in any department could have written the passage, it is not finished. Return to the anchor, find the decision it would have made (a verdict, a named object, a discrimination, a plain phrase, a shorter landing, a first-person concession) and make it. Methods and results are exempt where convention protects clarity; there the test is whether every noun keeps its referent. The test never licenses adding wit, coinage, or semicolons as markers. It asks for a decision, not a decoration.

## Do not

- Claim that voice calibration ran if you did not read the anchors or this file; label the calibration `PORTABLE`, and `FALLBACK` if you used neither.
- Infer that a text was or was not machine-written from its style. These are drafting rules and review cues, not authorship tests.
- Manufacture the voice: forced humour, coinage as garnish, semicolons as a quota, or theory as credentials all miss in the same way generic prose misses.
